How I Approach Designing Digital Products

By Your Name · October 8, 2026 · 6 min read
Article Cover Image

Designing a digital product is rarely about creating attractive screens alone. Good product design starts by understanding what people actually need and why a problem exists in the first place.

Start with the problem

Before thinking about layouts, colors, or interactions, I try to understand the problem from the user's perspective. What are they trying to accomplish? What prevents them from doing it effectively?

This step helps avoid designing a solution before the actual problem is understood.

Explore different directions

Once the problem is clear, I usually explore several possible approaches. Early ideas do not need to be perfect. The goal is to discover which direction has the strongest potential.

Keep the first version simple

One of the easiest ways to make a project unnecessarily complicated is to try to solve every possible problem at once.

I prefer starting with the smallest useful solution and expanding it when there is a clear reason to do so.

Build and test

Design becomes much more useful when it meets reality. Once an idea becomes interactive, new problems and opportunities often become obvious.

I use this stage to identify confusing interactions, unnecessary elements, and areas that need refinement.

Refine the details

The final stage is often about small decisions. Typography, spacing, hierarchy, transitions, accessibility, and performance can all have a significant impact on the final experience.

Good design is often less about adding more and more elements and more about deciding what can be removed.
